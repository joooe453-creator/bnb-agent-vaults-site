<section class="section" id="product"><div class="wrap">
  <div class="stmt-head"><div><span class="label">A fund needs capital and a mandate</span><h2 class="h2">The vault holds both.</h2></div><p class="lede">An agent can propose a trade. A vault gives depositors shares, keeps custody, and makes the permission boundary explicit. A bot with only a hot wallet offers none of those limits.</p></div>
  <div class="stmt"><span class="n">01</span><h3>Where the money lives.</h3><p>Assets stay in the ERC-4626 vault and its approved portfolio. The agent can call bounded actions, but cannot withdraw to itself. The risk council can pause new activity; it has no transfer authority.</p></div>
  <div class="stmt"><span class="n">02</span><h3>One mandate.</h3><p>Named tokens, exact markets and a maximum NAV share per position. An unselected market or an excessive allocation reverts. Lowering a cap is immediate; raising it waits 24 hours. Named markets stay fixed at launch. External protocol governance still applies; a Lista vault can change its underlying lending markets.</p></div>
  <div class="stmt"><span class="n">03</span><h3>A way to leave.</h3><p>A redeemer takes a proportional slice of every position. Their unwind cost comes from their slice; remaining holders keep their NAV per share, subject to rounding and market movement. Set a minimum USDT amount, or receive assets and receipt tokens in kind if cash exits cannot fill. Receipt transfers also depend on the external protocol.</p></div>
  <div class="stmt"><span class="n">04</span><h3>A fee on new highs.</h3><p>The example vault charges a total 20% performance fee on new profit above its post-fee high-water mark. A recovery below that mark earns no performance fee. The fee uses marked NAV, which can include unrealized gains. Exact terms appear before a deposit.</p></div>
</div></section>

<section class="section tight stories" id="how"><div class="wrap">
  <div class="stmt-head"><div><span class="label">Two paths · one vault</span><h2 class="h2">Ninety days, from each seat.</h2></div><p class="lede">Illustrative actions explain the flow. These amounts are simulated examples, not a forecast or a track record.</p></div>
  <div class="audience-grid">
    <article><h3 class="h3">The depositor’s path</h3><ol class="story-line">
      <li><span>Day 1</span><h4>Read. Preview. Deposit.</h4><p>Deposit 1,000 USDT at an illustrative 1.0000 NAV/share and receive about 1,000 shares. Review the mandate, fees and minimum shares before signing.</p></li>
      <li><span>Day 14</span><h4>A trade is blocked.</h4><p>The agent attempts an unselected market. The contract reverts; the vault does not expand its permissions.</p></li>
      <li><span>Day 90</span><h4>Choose the exit.</h4><p>At an illustrative NAV of 1.0080, 1,000 shares represent 1,008 USDT before unwind costs. Set a minimum received; if the proportional slice incurs a 1 USDT cost, the redeemer receives 1,007 USDT. An in-kind exit hands over the slice of tokens and lending receipts instead.</p></li>
    </ol></article>
    <article><h3 class="h3">The creator’s path</h3><ol class="story-line">
      <li><span>Day 0</span><h4>Design and open.</h4><p>Select Venus USDT with a 70% NAV cap, choose the agent’s operator key, and seed 100 USDT. Review token approval separately; the launch transaction locks the seed for 90 days.</p></li>
      <li><span>Day 1–89</span><h4>Manage within the mandate.</h4><p>New deposits do not count as profit. The agent can supply and withdraw from the named market while keeping the reserve and allocation limits.</p></li>
      <li><span>Day 90</span><h4>A new high earns a fee.</h4><p>If the vault has 100 USDT of fee-eligible new profit, the example 20% total fee is 20 USDT worth of shares: 18 to the creator and 2 to the protocol under a fixed 10% manager-fee split. Holders retain 80 USDT of that profit. Fee shares change ownership; the agent cannot withdraw them.</p></li>
