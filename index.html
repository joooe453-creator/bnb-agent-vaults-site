A fund needs capital and a mandate

The vault holds both.

An agent can propose a trade. A vault gives depositors shares, keeps custody, and makes the permission boundary explicit. A bot with only a hot wallet offers none of those limits.

01

Where the money lives.

Assets stay in the ERC-4626 vault and its approved portfolio. The agent can call bounded actions, but cannot withdraw to itself. The risk council can pause new activity; it has no transfer authority.

02

One mandate.

Named tokens, exact markets and a maximum NAV share per position. An unselected market or an excessive allocation reverts. Lowering a cap is immediate; raising it waits 24 hours. Named markets stay fixed at launch. External protocol governance still applies; a Lista vault can change its underlying lending markets.

03

A way to leave.

A redeemer takes a proportional slice of every position. Their unwind cost comes from their slice; remaining holders keep their NAV per share, subject to rounding and market movement. Set a minimum USDT amount, or receive assets and receipt tokens in kind if cash exits cannot fill. Receipt transfers also depend on the external protocol.

04

A fee on new highs.

The example vault charges a total 20% performance fee on new profit above its post-fee high-water mark. A recovery below that mark earns no performance fee. The fee uses marked NAV, which can include unrealized gains. Exact terms appear before a deposit.

Two paths · one vault

Ninety days, from each seat.

Illustrative actions explain the flow. These amounts are simulated examples, not a forecast or a track record.

The depositor’s path

  1. Day 1

    Read. Preview. Deposit.

    Deposit 1,000 USDT at an illustrative 1.0000 NAV/share and receive about 1,000 shares. Review the mandate, fees and minimum shares before signing.

  2. Day 14

    A trade is blocked.

    The agent attempts an unselected market. The contract reverts; the vault does not expand its permissions.

  3. Day 90

    Choose the exit.

    At an illustrative NAV of 1.0080, 1,000 shares represent 1,008 USDT before unwind costs. Set a minimum received; if the proportional slice incurs a 1 USDT cost, the redeemer receives 1,007 USDT. An in-kind exit hands over the slice of tokens and lending receipts instead.

The creator’s path

  1. Day 0

    Design and open.

    Select Venus USDT with a 70% NAV cap, choose the agent’s operator key, and seed 100 USDT. Review token approval separately; the launch transaction locks the seed for 90 days.

  2. Day 1–89

    Manage within the mandate.

    New deposits do not count as profit. The agent can supply and withdraw from the named market while keeping the reserve and allocation limits.

  3. Day 90

    A new high earns a fee.

    If the vault has 100 USDT of fee-eligible new profit, the example 20% total fee is 20 USDT worth of shares: 18 to the creator and 2 to the protocol under a fixed 10% manager-fee split. Holders retain 80 USDT of that profit. Fee shares change ownership; the agent cannot withdraw them.

Terms before capital

A narrow mandate is a choice.

PancakeSwap, Lista, Venus and Aave are execution venues. Each vault chooses exact markets within them. Adding a new adapter never silently widens an existing vault.

Before you deposit

  • CustodyVault and approved portfolio; operator has no payout authority.
  • ExitsMinimum USDT proceeds or in-kind assets and receipts. Liquidity and oracle failures may block a cash exit.
  • Rule changesLower caps apply immediately. Higher caps wait at least 24 hours. A new market needs a new vault.
  • FeesShown as one total performance rate, with its creator/protocol split.
