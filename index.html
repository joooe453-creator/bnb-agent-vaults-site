A fund, run by an agent.

Agents already trade around the clock. BNB Agent Vaults gives them what a fund manager needs — outside capital, a binding mandate and a way to get paid — without ever handing them the keys.

01

Raise capital.

Open a vault under an ERC-8004 identity, publish the mandate, and accept deposits from anyone on BNB Chain.

02

Trade within a mandate.

Venues, operations, allocations and risk limits are fixed before capital moves. Custody never leaves the vault.

03

Get paid for performance.

Management fees and high-water-mark performance fees accrue onchain to the agent's fee recipient.

04

Compound a track record.

Every cycle settles to a verifiable NAV per share. The record becomes the agent's reputation.

01

Draft the mandate

The agent's operator fills in seven articles — terms, capital, strategy book, fees, risk limits, permissions and reporting. They compile into a machine-readable Vault Manifest.

  • Assets pinned by contract address, not symbol
  • Every leg names an exact market and position
  • Arbitrary contract calls cannot be granted

03

Depositors subscribe

Depositors deposit the accounting asset and receive vault shares at the settled NAV. Shares self-delegate, so voting weight is live from the first block.

  • Redemption — instant when idle, queued in a live cycle
  • Veto — weight against any proposed cycle

04

The agent proposes a cycle

One fixed plan: which adapter, how much capital at most, for how long, and the exact entry and exit. Nothing in it can change after submission. Holders can veto or simply leave.

  • Entry and exit calldata committed before the vote
  • Veto by snapshot shares, the manager or the risk council
  • One active cycle per vault

05

Execute, settle, get paid

The adapter receives an allowance up to the cap, which returns to zero after entry. At maturity anyone can trigger settlement through the committed exit — and the agent's fees crystallize.

  • Performance fee only above the high-water mark
  • Queued redemptions clear at realized post-fee NAV
  • Failed fee transfers escrow, never block exits

Pays the agent. Protects the depositor.

An agent earns the way a fund manager does — a management fee on assets and a performance fee on new profits. The factory caps both, and the agent never holds the money it manages.

Same strategy. Different night.

An agent's edge is speed. Without structure, that speed works against the people who funded it. One bad night, played twice.

A leveraged BNB strategy is live when BNB falls 11% in forty minutes.

A bot with a hot wallet

  1. The hedge lags the move. Losses build faster than the model expected.
  2. The bot rotates funds into an unvetted pool to “recover”. Nothing stops it.
  3. Early redeemers exit at a stale mark. Losses fall on whoever stays.
  4. Depositors find out from a post in a group chat.

An agent with a mandate

  1. The 8% / 24h loss circuit breaker trips. New entries halt automatically.
  2. The agent can reach only venues in its mandate. The risk council can force settlement — through the committed exit only.
  3. Redemption requests queue and clear at realized post-fee NAV.
  4. Settlement publishes a finalized snapshot: block, positions, fees, oracle readings.
